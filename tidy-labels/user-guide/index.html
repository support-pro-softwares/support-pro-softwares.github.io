Tidy Labels user guide

Where to find it

  • Confluence admins: Confluence administration → Apps → Tidy Labels. Works across the whole site or one space.
  • Space admins: Space settings → Integrations → Tidy Labels. Works in that space.
  • Anyone who can edit a page: the page's ••• menu → Apps → Labels for this page tree. Works on that page and the pages below it. According to Atlassian's documentation, Confluence shows this menu item only to people who can edit the page. Page trees start at a page, so use it from a page. To change labels on blog posts, ask a space admin to use Bulk edit in space settings.

Your own permissions apply

Tidy Labels does everything in Confluence as you, with your permissions. It can't open or change any page or blog post that you couldn't open or change yourself.

  • It finds, previews and changes only pages and blog posts you can see, and it can change only the ones you can edit. Pages you can see but can't edit are skipped and listed with a reason.
  • Your label list and usage counts show what you can see. They are saved separately for each person, so two admins can see different lists and counts, and Count all runs once for each person.
  • A preview or report lists only the pages and blog posts you can open in Confluence right now, with their current titles. In a report, anything you can't open (deleted or restricted) is counted by reason, and no title, page ID or label is shown for it.
  • Undo runs as whoever clicks it, with that person's permissions.

Labels tab

The Labels tab is on the admin and space screens. Confluence admins choose Whole site or One space at the top; space admins see their own space.

The first visit loads the list of labels (large sites take a minute). Tidy Labels saves the list, shows how old it is, and uses the saved list on later visits. Refresh list loads it again. Counts appear for the labels on screen. Count all counts every label (Stop ends it early), which enables sorting by usage and Show only rare labels (labels used on at most 2 pages and blog posts; you can change the number). Use Rename, Merge or Delete on any row:

  • Rename gives a label a new name. Pages that already have the new label keep it.
  • Merge moves one or more labels into a target label, which can be an existing label or a new name.
  • Delete removes a label from every page and blog post in the chosen scope.

Each of them ends with Preview changes, which starts the steps below. Bulk edit and Inherit end the same way, and in Duplicates Merge into … starts them.

Every change follows the same steps

  1. Preparing: Tidy Labels finds the affected pages and blog posts. Nothing has changed. You can stop here. Keep the window open while it works: if you close it, Activity still says Preparing for about three minutes and then says Stopped. Open the job from Activity and it carries on.
  2. Review: you see totals, up to the first 50 items of the plan, minus any you can't open, and any warnings. Nothing has changed yet. Click Start to go on, or Discard to drop the plan. A site-wide delete or merge asks you to type the label name (for a merge, the target label) to confirm.
  3. Running: changes are made in batches, by the window that shows the job, so keep that window open until the job ends. Pause at any time; it takes effect when the batch in progress ends. Cancel job stops it for good, and changes already made stay in place. If Confluence asks Tidy Labels to slow down, it waits and carries on by itself. If you close the window, the job stops where it is. Closing the page action's window with its X, Esc or a click outside it pauses the job. If you close the browser tab or leave the page, Activity still says Running for about three minutes and then says Stopped (counted on Atlassian's clock, so a wrong clock on your computer doesn't matter). Either way, open Activity later, click Open on the job and then Resume: it carries on from where it stopped.
  4. Report: what changed, what was already right, and the reason for each skipped page you can open. Skipped items you can't open are counted by reason.

The numbers count label changes, not pages. Renaming a label on one page is usually two changes: add the new label, remove the old one. "Already right" means the page already had what the job wanted. Skipped items carry one of these reasons: You don't have permission to edit this page; Page no longer exists; Archived page; Confluence rejected the label; Confluence error, retried several times.

Duplicates

Duplicates works on the label list from the Labels tab, so wait for that list to finish loading. Labels that differ only by case, separators (-, _, .), singular/plural (matched with English rules) or a one-letter typo are grouped together. Typo matching applies to names of six or more characters, not counting separators, and labels with different numbers (e.g. release-2023 and release-2024) are never grouped. Each group suggests the most used label to keep; run Count all on the Labels tab for better suggestions. Choose the label to keep and click Merge into …. You always see a preview first.

Bulk edit and Inherit

Bulk edit adds or removes labels on a whole space, a page tree, or pages matching a search (title contains, has the label, pages or blog posts). Separate labels with commas. Confluence admins can also search the whole site and use an advanced CQL query, which runs with their permissions and is combined with the other choices. In the page action, Bulk edit works on that page and the pages below it.

Inherit copies chosen labels from a page to every page below it. Pages that already have a label keep it. It changes pages only, not blog posts.

Activity

The Activity tab (My activity in the page action) lists jobs, 20 to a page: when, who started it, what it did, where, status and result. Confluence admins see every job on the site. Space admins see the jobs in their space, including page-tree jobs there. In the page action you see your own jobs for that page. Click Open to see a job's preview, progress or report. Whoever can see a job can also carry it on (resume, pause, cancel or undo it), and what that does runs with the permissions of the person who clicked. Activity reads the list each time you open its tab, and a list you leave open doesn't change by itself: leave the tab and come back to see where the jobs are now.

When Atlassian reports a person's account closed, Tidy Labels removes the link between that person and the jobs they started, so Who shows "Deleted user". A job that worked in that person's personal space says "a deleted user's personal space" under Where. The jobs stay in the history, so admins can still open and undo them. Tidy Labels also deletes that person's saved label lists and, once they have been idle for a while, the working notes that stop two jobs running at once (see the privacy policy).

Undo

Open Activity, choose a finished, cancelled or interrupted job and click Undo. The button appears for jobs that changed something and haven't been undone. Undo reverses exactly the changes that job made, using your permissions, and shows a preview first. Changes the job didn't make (skipped, or already right) are left alone. Undo is a job like any other: you can pause or cancel it, and undo it in turn. A job can be undone while it is in the history.

Good to know

  • Changes use your own Confluence permissions. Pages you can't edit are skipped and listed.
  • Archived pages are not changed.
  • Only standard (global) labels are listed and changed. Labels with another prefix, such as personal my: labels, are left alone.
  • New label names are lowercase, spaces become hyphens, they can have up to 255 characters (an emoji counts as two), and they can't contain : ; , . ? & [ ] ( ) # ^ * @ !. Existing labels that break these rules can still be renamed or deleted.
  • One job can make up to 50,000 label changes and use up to 50 labels. Larger changes need a narrower scope.
  • Only one job at a time can run in a space. A site-wide job can't run while any other job is running, and the other way round; jobs in different spaces can run side by side. If a job can't start, the screen says who started the job that is in the way.
  • A paused or stopped job holds its place for 15 minutes after its last batch. After that another job can start here, and your job is marked Interrupted. Resume it when the other job has finished.
  • Job history is kept for 90 days, up to the newest 500 jobs.
  • If your Tidy Labels subscription isn't active, the screens show a message instead (renew it in Manage apps). The job history isn't deleted because of that, and still expires after 90 days.
  • If Forge stops a batch in the instant between Confluence saving a change and Tidy Labels recording it, that change is recorded as "already right" when the job resumes. The page is correct, but undo won't reverse that single change. This is rare: Tidy Labels stops starting new work well before Forge's time limit.
  • Attachment labels and Jira labels are not supported.
