Tidy Labels – privacy policy

Effective 15 October 2026. Provided by Pro Softwares ("we").

This policy describes what the Tidy Labels app for Confluence Cloud (version 1) stores, where, for how long and who can see it, and what the app reports to Atlassian and erases about people. It describes how the app works. It is not a certification and not a statement of compliance with any privacy law or standard.

What Tidy Labels stores, in Atlassian's Forge storage for your site only:

  • job records: the type of change, the label names involved, where it applies (the whole site, a space, or a page tree and its start page), the search settings typed for a bulk edit (title words, a label, the content type and, for Confluence admins, an advanced CQL query), the search query the app built from them, who started it (their Atlassian account ID), when, and result counts;
  • the content IDs of the pages and blog posts each job planned to change, and the result for each change;
  • the titles of up to 50 pages and blog posts shown in each job's preview, with the labels to add or remove. The app stores these titles when it plans the job. They are not what people see: whenever anyone opens a preview or report, the app asks Confluence for the current titles as that person;
  • a saved list of label names and usage counts for the site or a space, kept separately for each person who opens the site-wide or space screen, with a small note that points to the list and names its owner (their account ID);
  • working notes that stop two things happening at once. The note of a job, for the whole site or for a space, says that a job is running there, and holds the job ID, the account ID of the person running it and the time of its last activity. The notes of single steps (planning, a batch, a pause, building a saved label list) hold only a time; the one for a saved label list also has its owner's account ID in its name;
  • the app's own records of its personal data reporting (see below): for each person, their account ID with the time of the last report, a mark while the erasure for a closed account is unfinished, and a mark when Atlassian refuses to take an ID; and one record, with no account ID in it, that remembers when Atlassian last accepted accounts.

What it does not store: page or blog post content, comments, attachments, email addresses, names or profile pictures. Where a name appears on screen (for example in Activity), Atlassian shows it from the account ID. The app stores nothing in your browser.

Personal data: the app stores Atlassian account IDs (the identifier Atlassian gives each user):

  • the person who started each job;
  • the owner of a personal space that a job works in. The key of a personal space is ~ followed by an identifier of its owner, and a job stores the key of its space, so it names that person even if they never used the app;
  • the person running a job, in the note of the job;
  • the person a saved label list belongs to, in the names of the list, of the note that points to it and of the note of the step that builds it;
  • the app's own reporting records that name a person, listed above.

Page titles, label names, search text and the keys of ordinary spaces are typed or chosen by your people and can contain personal information. The app can't tell, so it neither reports nor erases them. They expire with the job.

Where it lives: inside Atlassian's cloud, in Forge storage that is scoped to your site ("Runs on Atlassian" hosting). The app has no servers or databases of its own and makes no calls outside Atlassian. None of the data described here is sent to us or to anyone other than Atlassian, who hosts it. The only thing that reaches us from the app is its log lines (see "Who can see it"). Once an hour a scheduled job of the app calls Atlassian's personal data reporting API, which is an Atlassian service (see "Reporting to Atlassian and erasure"). According to Atlassian's documentation, an admin can control where Forge storage data is held (Atlassian's data residency controls), and the data is encrypted and stored under Atlassian's cloud policies.

How long:

  • Job records and everything stored with them (planned changes, results, the preview, pause and cancel requests) are marked to expire 90 days after the job was created. A job that is discarded, or that has nothing to change, is deleted at once.
  • When a new job (or an undo job) is created, the app also trims the history to the newest 500 jobs, oldest first. It keeps a running job, the original of an undo job that isn't finished, and a job that another window is working on at that moment (the next trim tries again). A trim has a time limit like any other step, so when many jobs are over the limit it removes some now and the rest at the next one. It also deletes the working note of a job that it removes, if the job still has one.
  • A person's saved label list, and the small note that points to it, expire 90 days after the scan that built the list started.
  • The app's reporting records: the record of the last report expires 30 days after it was written (the app writes it again at each report, about every 7 days, so it goes about a month after a person is no longer stored). The mark about a refused ID expires after 30 days. The record of when Atlassian last accepted accounts expires after 7 days. The mark about a closed account stays until its erasure is finished, and is then deleted.
  • Working notes. The note of a single step is deleted when the step ends, and one that a stopped step left behind is deleted by the hourly job once it is an hour old. The note of a job, which stops two jobs running at once in a space (or on the whole site), has no expiry date of its own. The app deletes it when the job finishes or is cancelled and when the history trim removes the job, and a later job takes it over once it has been idle for 15 minutes. The hourly job also deletes a note that has been idle for 15 minutes and whose job is finished or gone. The note of a paused or abandoned job stays for as long as the job is kept, at most 90 days, and holds an account ID until then. It goes earlier when the person it names is reported closed (the hourly job then deletes it once it has been idle for 15 minutes), and when the app is uninstalled.
  • According to Atlassian's documentation, Atlassian's storage can take up to 48 hours to remove an expired item. The app stops showing a job once its 90 days are up.
  • If you uninstall Tidy Labels, Atlassian deletes the app's stored data for your site under its own retention rules. We keep no copy. Atlassian may keep backups under its own policies.

Reporting to Atlassian and erasure: the app uses Atlassian's personal data reporting API, as Atlassian asks of apps that store personal data. A scheduled job of the app runs every hour, as the app and with no user. It reads and writes only Forge storage and calls only Atlassian's reporting API. It never calls Confluence, and it runs whether or not the subscription is active.

  • What is sent, and to whom. For each account ID the app stores, it sends Atlassian the account ID and one time: the earliest time the app stored that person's data. A job counts from the time it was created, and the app keeps the earliest time of all the places that hold the ID. A person who appears only in the note of a job, or who only owns saved label lists, has no job to count from: the time sent is then that of the note's last activity, or of the start of the list's scan. Nothing else is sent: no names, email addresses, page titles, label names, search text or job contents. It goes to Atlassian only.
  • How often. Each account is sent at most once every 7 days (Atlassian's default cycle), so in practice every 7 days and normally at most an hour late. Up to 90 accounts go in one call. A run works for about 12 seconds and carries on at the next run, so a site with very many accounts may need some hours to report them all for the first time. If Atlassian is busy or a call fails, the app tries again at the next run.
  • What each run does, in this order. It deletes the old notes of single steps, and the notes of jobs that are finished or gone (see "How long"). It finishes the erasure for accounts that Atlassian already reported closed, using at most half of the run's time. It reports the accounts that are due. Then it erases for any account that this report found closed. Erasure for accounts already known to be closed comes before the reporting, so a problem with the reporting can't hold it back.
  • When Atlassian says an account is closed. The app learns this when it next reports that account, so within about a week. It then erases the following, in that run or in the next ones (an account with very many jobs may need several):
    • the link to the person in every job they started. The job stays in the history, so other admins can still open it and undo it, but Activity shows "Deleted user";
    • the key of that person's personal space in every job that works there, whoever started the job, and the stored search that contains it. Activity then says "a deleted user's personal space". A job that is still being prepared keeps the key until its preparation ends;
    • the person's saved label lists, whole, with the notes that point to them;
    • the note of a job that names the person, once it has been idle for 15 minutes. A running or paused job that held it is marked Interrupted and can be resumed;
    • last, the app's own records about that person.
  • When Atlassian says "updated". Nothing changes, because the app stores no name, email address or profile picture.
  • An ID that Atlassian refuses. The app reports in two groups, one after the other. The first holds the account IDs that Atlassian itself gave the app: the people who started jobs, who run them, or who own saved label lists. The second holds the owners of personal spaces, whose IDs the app reads out of the key of the space. In our testing, the key of a current account's personal space was ~ and the account ID without its colon and hyphens (Atlassian does not document this). The app puts them back, so that it sends the ID Atlassian knows, and a person who is in both groups is sent once, in the first. For a key that holds something else, such as a user name, the app can't tell whether the text is an account ID, and Atlassian may refuse it. The second group is reported only when every account of the first that is due has been sent, so a refused ID can't hold back a real account. If Atlassian refuses a call, the app sends the accounts again in smaller groups until it finds the ID that is refused, and reports the others in the same run. It skips a refused ID for 30 days and then tries it again. It marks an ID as refused only when it is clear that the ID is at fault, for example when Atlassian accepted other accounts sent with it. For the owner of a personal space it counts as well that Atlassian accepted accounts of the first group, in this run or in the last 7 days (the app remembers that for 7 days, in a record with no account ID in it). Otherwise it marks nobody and tries again at the next run. If Atlassian seems to refuse everything, the app stops after a few calls. While an ID is skipped the app can't learn that the account is closed, so nothing is erased early for it: its jobs and saved label lists expire after 90 days as usual, and its working notes follow the rules under "How long".
  • What stays. Erasure removes account IDs, not the work. These stay until their job expires after 90 days: label names, the content IDs and results of each job, counts, page titles in stored previews, the search settings typed for bulk edits, and the keys of ordinary spaces. They are not account IDs, but your people could have typed a name or an ID into them, and the app can't tell.

Who can see it: only people using the app on your site, through its screens, and only what their Confluence permissions allow.

  • Confluence admins see every job on the site in Activity: who started it (or "Deleted user"), what it did, where, and result counts.
  • Space admins see the jobs in their space, including page-tree jobs there.
  • Someone using "Labels for this page tree" sees their own jobs for that page.
  • A job's preview and report show only the pages and blog posts the viewer can open in Confluence right now, with their current titles. Items the viewer can't open are left out of the preview and appear in the report only as counts per result code, with no ID, title or label.
  • Each person's saved label list is built with that person's permissions and used only for that person.
  • If another job is blocking yours, the screen shows who started it.

We can't read your stored data. According to Atlassian's documentation, only the app itself can read and write it, and this app makes no calls outside Atlassian. The only thing that reaches us from the app is its log lines. A log line holds the event name, job IDs, the type of change (rename, merge, delete, bulk add, bulk remove or inherit), the kind of place it ran in (whole site, space or page tree), status and result codes, yes-or-no flags (for example whether a batch was rate limited), counts (for example how many label changes a batch made), the name of the app function that failed unexpectedly, the class name of an error (never its message) and HTTP status codes. A line is also written when a step can't delete its working note at the end; it names the job when the step belonged to a job. A log line never holds an account ID, page title, label name, space key or search text. The hourly reporting job writes one line per run with counts only: accounts stored, due, reported, updated, closed and refused; jobs, saved label lists and working notes erased for closed accounts; old notes of single steps and notes of finished or gone jobs deleted; pieces left for the next run or that failed; closed accounts still to finish; and how the run ended. When Atlassian refuses an ID or a call, a line says so with the HTTP status or a code, and with a flag if it was about the owner of a personal space. The hourly job never logs an account ID, and its own lines hold no job ID.

Analytics and tracking: none. The app has no analytics, tracking or advertising code.

What we receive outside the app: the emails you send to support@prosoftwares.dev, and the licensing and purchase details that Atlassian shares with Marketplace vendors under its own Marketplace terms.

Your rights and contact: questions or requests about the data the app holds: support@prosoftwares.dev. We have no way to look up or delete single records in your site's storage. The app erases a person's data by itself when Atlassian reports that person's account closed (see above). Otherwise job data and saved label lists expire on their own (see "How long"), and a Confluence admin can remove all of the app's stored data by uninstalling it.
