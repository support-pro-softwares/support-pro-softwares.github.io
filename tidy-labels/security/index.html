Tidy Labels for Confluence

Data and security statement

In short. Tidy Labels runs entirely on Atlassian's Forge platform. It talks only to Confluence, as the person using it, and to Atlassian's own storage and personal data reporting service. It has no servers of its own and sends nothing to us except log lines made of IDs, codes and counts. It can do only what the person using it is allowed to do in Confluence. It keeps job history and saved label lists for a limited time, plus working notes that stop two things happening at once. The app deletes a note when its job or step ends. An hourly job deletes the notes that a stopped step left behind and the note of a job that is finished or gone. It stores Atlassian account IDs, but no names, email addresses, page content or credentials. It reports those account IDs to Atlassian on a regular cycle and erases a person's data when Atlassian reports that person's account closed. The details follow.

This statement describes Tidy Labels version 1. Each answer comes from the app's code and manifest, except where it says "Atlassian's documentation", which marks a statement about the Forge platform that Atlassian makes. It is not a certification and not a statement of compliance with any law or standard. The privacy policy is the one place that describes retention, reporting and erasure: where this page points to it, the policy is the answer.

  • Hosting: Atlassian Forge, built to the "Runs on Atlassian" model: all code runs, and all data is stored, on Atlassian's platform. No external servers, no egress, no remote calls. The manifest declares no remotes and no external permissions, and the app has no web triggers or event triggers. Its one scheduled trigger runs the personal data job described under Personal data. The screens are built with Atlassian's UI Kit; the app serves no web pages, scripts or files of its own. Data flow: the person's browser talks to the app's Forge function on Atlassian's platform, and that function calls Confluence's own REST APIs as that person and reads and writes Forge storage. The hourly scheduled function is a second, separate function. It runs as the app, with no user, reads and writes Forge storage, calls Atlassian's personal data reporting API, and never calls Confluence. No other service is involved.
  • Data stored: see the data inventory below. It is kept in Forge storage (the key-value store), scoped to the installation. Atlassian's documentation says only the app can read and write its stored data and that it cannot read data from other sites or environments. The app stores nothing in the browser.
  • Encryption: done by Atlassian's platform. Atlassian's documentation says Forge storage data is transferred, encrypted and stored according to Atlassian cloud's data encryption policies. The app adds no encryption of its own. (The base64url text in keys is an encoding, not encryption.)
  • Storage location: Forge storage only, hosted by Atlassian. Atlassian's documentation says an admin can control where that data is stored (Atlassian's data residency controls). The app sends no stored data to us or to any third party. The only account data it sends anywhere is to Atlassian's personal data reporting API (see Personal data).
  • Retention: see "How long" in the privacy policy, which covers expiry, the 500-job limit, working notes, the expiry lag, uninstall and backups. The inventory below gives the expiry of each kind of record.
  • Personal data: the records that hold an Atlassian account ID are marked in the inventory below. The app stores no names, email addresses or profile pictures. Page titles, label names, search text and the keys of ordinary spaces come from your users, can contain personal information, and are neither reported nor erased. What is reported to Atlassian, how often, what is erased when Atlassian reports an account closed, what happens to an ID that Atlassian refuses, and what stays are described under "Reporting to Atlassian and erasure" in the privacy policy. What the policy leaves out:
    • Scope and trigger. The scope report:personal-data, used only by the hourly job. One scheduled trigger, hourly, runs the function privacy. It runs as the app with no user, touches only Forge storage and Atlassian's reporting API, never calls Confluence, and does not check the subscription. No screen can call it.
    • Account IDs sent. The account ID unknown is never sent. The IDs go in two groups, one after the other, each with its own batches, splits and cap. The first is the IDs that the platform gave the app: the creator of a job, the holder of a scope lock, the owner of a saved label list and of its lease. The second is the owner of a personal space, read from the key of the space (~ and an ID). QA saw that the key of a current account's personal space is ~ and the account ID without its colon and hyphens (Atlassian does not document the key), so the app puts them back: when the text after the ~ is letters and digits that end with exactly 32 hex digits, the ID is the part before those 32 digits, a colon and the 32 digits as a UUID (8-4-4-4-12). That is the ID the platform gives for the same account, and the same reading finds the jobs to erase when Atlassian reports it closed. Any other text after the ~ (a legacy ID of 24 hex digits, a user name) is sent as it is, and nothing says that it is an account ID, so Atlassian may refuse it. The second group is sent only when every ID of the first that is due has been sent. An ID that is in both groups, such as the admin who started a job in their own personal space, is sent once, in the first, with the older of its two times.
    • Failures. A rate limit (429), a server error or any failure with no client-error status ends the run, is logged as a code, and is tried again at the next run. Nothing is recorded for the accounts of a failed call.
    • Refused IDs. When Atlassian refuses a call with a client error other than 429, the app sends the batch again in halves, both halves before either is split further, so what Atlassian takes is written down early. An ID is marked as refused (a privacy:unreportable record) only when something shows that the ID is at fault: Atlassian took other accounts of its batch, whatever status it refused this one with; or, for an ID of the second group, Atlassian took IDs of the first group in the same run or within the last 7 days (the privacy:proof record below); or the ID was the whole batch and Atlassian answered 400 or 422. For any other client error on a lone ID (for example 401, 403, 404 or 408) with none of that proof, and when Atlassian refuses a whole batch, nobody is marked: the run stops, logs the status, and the next run tries again. A stall in the second group ends the run too, but only after every ID of the first group that is due was sent. The split stops once four IDs were refused on their own and none was taken, so a refusal of everything costs 19 calls for a batch of 90 and not the run's whole time budget. Where IDs of the first group were taken, the split of the second group has no such stop, because the refusals are then the IDs' own.
    • The proof. After the first group, whenever Atlassian took at least one of its IDs (also when a later call of the same run failed), the app writes privacy:proof: a time and nothing else, with no account ID in it, expiring after 7 days. Every run reads it before it sends anything. A record that is 7 days old or older, that lies in the future or that cannot be read proves nothing, and a run in which Atlassian took none of the first group writes none. A failed write is counted in failed and does not stop the run.
    • This statement is not a claim of compliance with any privacy law or standard.
  • Access control:
    • Every Confluence read and write runs as the signed-in person (asUser). The app never calls Confluence as itself (asApp) and has no credentials of its own, so effects never exceed that person's Confluence permissions. Undo runs as whoever clicks it. The hourly function makes no Confluence call at all.
    • Each request is bound to the screen it came from. The app takes the screen (module) from the context Forge supplies, and for the space and page-tree screens the space or page too, never from the request; a request that names another space or page is refused. The site-wide settings page needs Confluence admin rights: the app checks them on every call from that page (GET /wiki/rest/api/user/current?expand=operations, the administer operation on the application), and site scope can only be requested from that page. The space screen can act only on its own space and on page trees inside it. The page action can act only on the page it was opened from (which must be in the same space) and on that page's descendants.
    • Who gets which screen: the site-wide page is for Confluence admins and the space page is a Confluence space-settings page for space admins. The page action carries a display condition so that Confluence shows it only to people who can edit the page (Atlassian's documentation says the condition checks the person's permission on the page). None of them gives more than the person's own permissions, because everything runs as that person.
    • Jobs are visible by screen. The site-wide page lists every job. The space page lists jobs whose scope is that space, including page-tree jobs there. The page action lists only the person's own jobs for that page. Any other job ID answers "not found", so IDs reveal nothing. A job's creator appears through Atlassian's user display (or as "Deleted user" once erased), with the label names, scope and result counts. Whoever can see a job can also resume, pause, cancel or undo it, and whatever that does runs with the permissions of the person who clicked.
    • Preview items and report entries are re-checked for each viewer, the creator included. The app asks Confluence, as the viewer, for the current titles of the items. It shows only items the viewer can open, with the titles they see now; stored titles are never shown. Items the viewer can't open are left out of the preview and appear in the report only as counts per result code, with no ID, title or label.
    • A saved label list belongs to one person. It is built with that person's permissions and no other person's screen reads it.
    • If a job can't start because another job holds the scope lock, the screen shows who started that job (the account ID in the lock record, through Atlassian's user display).
    • The licence is checked on every call from a screen, except the start-up call that explains an inactive licence. The entry point for screens serves only the app's own named functions. The hourly function is a separate function that only the scheduled trigger points at, so no screen can call it.
  • Permissions requested (scopes in the manifest): read Confluence users (used to check admin rights), search, read labels, write labels, read pages, read blog posts, read spaces, app storage, and report personal data (used only by the hourly job, to send account IDs to Atlassian's personal data reporting API). Label writes are the only write access to Confluence: the app can't change page content, comments, attachments, restrictions or settings.
  • Input handling: every request is validated against a strict schema, and unknown fields are refused. Label names are checked against Confluence's rules, and content IDs must be digits only. Search values are escaped, and search operators are removed from typed title words. Advanced CQL is accepted from Confluence admins only. It is limited to 2,000 characters, must have balanced parentheses and no ORDER BY, is combined with the app's own type and scope clauses, and still runs as them.
  • Logging: structured log lines, which are the app's Forge logs. We can read them with Forge's logging tools, and they are the only information from your site that reaches us.
    • A line holds: the event name; job IDs (and the ID of the job an undo reverses); the type of change (rename, merge, delete, bulk_add, bulk_remove or inherit); the kind of scope (site, space or tree, the last for a page tree); job status and result codes; yes-or-no flags (a batch rate limited or failed); counts (operations planned, label changes made, issues, chunk number, operations done); the name of the app function that failed unexpectedly; the class name of an error, never its message; and HTTP status codes.
    • lease_release_failed is written when a step cannot delete its lease at the end, after its work was done and saved. The step is not turned into an error: the lease goes stale after a minute and the next step takes it over, or the hourly job deletes it after an hour. The line holds the job ID when the lease was a job's and nothing else when it was a saved label list's, because the key of that lease holds an encoded account ID.
    • A line never holds an account ID, an encoded account ID, a page title, a label name, a space key, search text or page content.
    • The hourly reporting job writes one privacy_run line per run. Its fields are the counts stored, due, reported, updated, closed, unreportable, erasedJobs, erasedInventories, erasedLocks, erasedLeases, droppedLocks, skippedBusy, keptLocks, keptPlanning, failed and pending, plus ended (done, time, rate_limited, http_<status> or error) and error (an error class name, or null). erasedLeases counts the leases it deleted for being an hour old, and droppedLocks the scope locks it deleted because their job was gone or finished; erasedLocks is for the locks of closed accounts.
    • It also writes privacy_account_unreportable (the HTTP status only) when an ID is first marked as refused, privacy_report_failed (the code, and how many accounts were reported before the stop) when the reporting ends on a failure, and privacy_failed (the same counts as privacy_run) when the job cannot start. The first two carry "derived":true when they are about the second group (the owners of personal spaces) and have no such field otherwise. The privacy job's own lines hold no account ID, no encoded ID, no job ID and no error message; a lease_release_failed line written while it erases data follows the rule above.
  • Secrets: none. The app holds no credentials, tokens or API keys. Forge supplies the signed-in person's authorisation when the app calls Confluence, and the app's own authorisation for the reporting API.
  • Sub-processors: none besides Atlassian.
  • Known limitation (data integrity): if Forge stops a batch after Confluence applied a change but before the app saved its result, that change is recorded as "already right" when the job resumes. The page is correct, but undo won't reverse that one change. A safety margin before Forge's time limit makes this rare.
  • Vulnerability reports: support.prosoftwares@gmail.com. We acknowledge a report within one business day. We follow Atlassian's security bug fix policy for Marketplace cloud apps, which sets the longest time we may take to fix a vulnerability, by severity: critical (CVSS 9.0 or higher) 10 days, high (7.0 to 8.9) 4 weeks, medium (4.0 to 6.9) 12 weeks, low (below 4.0) 25 weeks, counted from the day it is reported or triaged. Atlassian's page gives days and weeks and does not say business days, so we count calendar days. The support policy's targets are for problems with the service and do not apply to vulnerabilities. The policy: https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/security-bugfix-policy/
  • Development practice: strict TypeScript, linting with no warnings allowed, automated tests with at least 90 % line coverage on all logic and data-access code (the test run fails below that), dependencies pinned to exact versions (Atlassian's Forge libraries, React and Zod), and a manual QA checklist run on a Confluence Cloud test site before release.

Data inventory

Every kind of record the app stores in Forge storage. A "list ID" is site or space-<spaceId>, then a dot, then a base64url encoding of the person's Atlassian account ID. An "encoded account ID" is that same encoding. Records that hold an account ID say so.

Record (key) What it holds How long it is kept
Job record job:<jobId> The request, who started it (account ID), progress and results. See "Job record, in detail" below. Expires 90 days after the job was created.
Operation chunks ops:<jobId>:<n> The planned changes, up to 500 per chunk: content ID, page or blog post, add or remove, and a number that points to a label name in the job record. Same as the job.
Result chunks res:<jobId>:<n> One result code per planned change (ok, noop, forbidden, not_found, archived, invalid, server_error, or empty while pending) and a small retry counter. Same as the job.
Stored preview preview:<jobId> Up to 50 items, each with the content ID, page or blog post, the page title, and the labels it adds or removes, plus warnings (a code, sometimes with a label name or a number). Capped at about 200 KB. Same as the job.
Pause or cancel request intent:<jobId> The word pause or cancel. Deleted when the request has been carried out. If it never is, it expires with the job.
Batch lease lease:<jobId> and lease:inv.<list ID> A timestamp. Held briefly while the app writes to a job (a planning step, a batch, a pause, a cancel, an undo) or builds or erases a label list. The label-list lease key holds the encoded account ID. Deleted when the step ends (a delete that fails is logged as lease_release_failed and does not fail the step). The hourly job deletes any lease that is an hour old or more. No TTL.
Scope lock lock:site and lock:space:<spaceId> The job ID, the account ID of the person running the job, and the time of its last heartbeat. No TTL. Deleted when the job finishes or is cancelled, and when the history trim removes the job. A later job takes over one with no heartbeat for 15 minutes. The hourly job deletes one with no heartbeat for 15 minutes whose job is finished or gone (counted in droppedLocks), and one whose holder was reported closed (erasedLocks). The lock of a job that is still alive, for example a paused one, stays as long as the job is kept, at most 90 days.
Saved label list inv:<list ID>:meta and inv:<list ID>:<n> For one person and one scope (the site, or one space): label names with usage counts (up to 500 rows per chunk), and a small header (state, progress, timestamps, the list ID). The list ID holds the encoded account ID. Expires 90 days after the scan started. Replaced by Refresh list.
Label-list index record invown:<encoded account ID>:<list ID> The owner's account ID in plain text, the list ID and the time the scan started. One per saved label list; it lets the privacy job find a person's lists without reading them. Expires 90 days after the scan started, like the list.
Report record privacy:reported:<encoded account ID> The account ID and the time it was last sent to Atlassian. None for an account Atlassian reported closed. Expires 30 days after it was written; written again at each report.
Closed-account mark privacy:pending:<encoded account ID> The account ID. Exists only while the erasure for an account Atlassian reported closed is unfinished. No expiry date, so the work can't be forgotten. Deleted by the app as the last step of the erasure.
Refused-ID mark privacy:unreportable:<encoded account ID> The account ID and the HTTP status with which Atlassian refused it (nothing else of Atlassian's answer). Expires 30 days after it was written; then the ID is tried again.
Proof record privacy:proof The time at which Atlassian last took IDs that the platform gave the app. No account ID, nothing else. Expires 7 days after it was written; written again whenever Atlassian takes such IDs.

Job record, in detail

  • Job ID, type, status, created and updated times, and the IDs of the original or undo job.
  • The account ID of the person who started it, until Atlassian reports that account closed (then it is cleared).
  • Where it applies: the whole site; a space with its ID and key; or a page tree with its space and start-page ID. The key of a personal space names its owner (~ and an identifier) and is replaced by a marker when that owner is reported closed.
  • What it does: the label names, and for a bulk edit the filter (typed title words, a label name, the content type and, for Confluence admins, advanced CQL).
  • The search query (CQL) the app built from the request. It contains the space key and is dropped when the key is erased. While planning, the job also holds the cursor into the search results.
  • Progress counts and result counts by code.

Notes

  • Titles. Page titles are stored only in the preview record, for at most 50 items per job. The app doesn't show them from storage: whenever anyone opens a preview or a report it asks Confluence for the current titles as that viewer. Reports store no titles.
  • Everything else about how long. Trimming the history, the expiry lag, working notes, uninstall and backups are in "How long" in the privacy policy.
